The Fueling Calendar

A 21-day timing tool. No pattern required. Free, permanently.

Some of what feels like overeating is your body catching up. Delayed meals, light intake, or uneven eating earlier in the day can create stronger hunger later — hunger that is harder to manage because it arrived late.

This calendar is not a diet. It is a timing tool. It does not tell you what to eat. It creates a structure that prevents the deficit from building in the first place — because once it builds, managing it is harder than preventing it.

It is free because timing is foundational. Behavioral pattern work is more effective when the physiological layer is not in the way.

Who this is for

01 — Oriented eaters

No strong behavioral pattern driving your eating. You may simply forget to eat or de-prioritize it in a busy day. The deficit builds quietly, without urgency, until it does not.

02 — Pattern + Fueling signal

You have an identified behavioral pattern — Soother, Drifter, Stabilizer, Seeker, or Social. Your pattern fires harder and earlier when a timing deficit is already in place. This removes the accelerant.

03 — High-output individuals

Your output level may be outpacing your intake. The math catches up — usually at night, usually with eating that feels urgent or hard to stop. That is not a discipline problem. It is a morning problem.

If you came here from your inventory result

Your primary pattern is still the behavioral driver. The Fueling signal your result identified means timing deficits are amplifying your pattern — making it fire harder and earlier than it otherwise would.

Start here. When the physiological layer is stable, your pattern work becomes more targeted and more effective. You may find that some of what felt like your pattern was partly the deficit.

Your pattern calendar is still the right tool for the underlying behavior. This calendar is the first step.

High-Output Variant

If your output level is high — four or more days a week of high-intensity or long-duration training — the standard calendar may not account for the demand your output creates. The high-output variant is built for that gap.

You are disciplined and willing to put in the work. The output side of your system is well-developed. The intake side may just need the same level of attention. When output drops — injury, travel, schedule change — the eating pattern does not automatically adjust. That is when things tend to feel out of control. It is almost always a fueling problem, not a discipline problem.
